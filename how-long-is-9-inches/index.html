Real-life use cases: ordering food and shopping online

Most people look up 9 inches when they are about to spend money on something and want to know what will show up at the door.

Take a 9-inch chicken sub sandwich. Most fast-food sub chains build their menus around a 6-inch half sub and a 12-inch footlong, while deli chains like Potbelly, Quiznos, and local sandwich shops sell a 9-inch medium sub. A 9-inch roll gives you half again as much food as a 6-inch sub, usually landing between 650 and 900 calories depending on the bread, meat, and cheese. If you spread your hand wide open from the tip of your thumb to the tip of your pinky finger, a 9-inch sandwich matches the full stretched hand span of an average adult man.

Ordering a 9-inch pizza trips people up for a different reason. Pizzerias label a 9-inch pie as a personal or small pizza and slice it into 4 or 6 wedges. Nine inches sounds close to a 12-inch medium pizza, so buyers often assume it is only a little smaller. Because a circle's area depends on the radius squared (π × r²), a 9-inch pizza has 63.6 square inches of crust and toppings, while a 12-inch pizza has 113.1 square inches. A 9-inch pizza gives you a little more than half the food of a 12-inch pizza, which makes it a solid meal for one person rather than something to split with a group.

You run into the same problem with novelty sweets and gift boxes, such as a 9-inch candy bar. A regular 1.55-ounce Hershey's bar at the grocery checkout is 5.37 inches long. A 9-inch specialty chocolate bar is almost as long as two regular bars laid end to end, and it usually weighs between 5 and 7 ounces.

Online product listings do the same thing. When you shop for a small frying pan, a tablet sleeve, a plant pot, or a chef's knife, the product photo fills the whole frame regardless of whether the item is 5 inches or 15 inches across. Holding your hands up to the 0-inch and 9-inch marks on the ruler above shows you the physical space that item will take up on your counter or in your bag before you buy it.

14 everyday objects that are an exact 9 inches

Every object in this first group is built to a fixed 9.0-inch specification. If you have one of these in a cupboard, desk drawer, or toolbox, you can use it as a direct physical stand-in for a 9-inch ruler.

1. A standard 9-inch pie plate or round cake pan

Nine inches is the standard diameter for American round bakeware, including glass Pyrex pie plates, round aluminum layer-cake pans, and 9x9 square brownie pans. When you use a baking pan to check a 9-inch length, measure across the center from the inside top wall to the opposite inside top wall. Bakeware makers always size pans by their interior walls so cake batters and pie crusts fit the recipe volume. The outer lip or glass handles will add an extra half-inch to an inch on the outside.

2. Twelve US pennies lined up in a straight row

Coins make great makeshift rulers because government mints strike them to strict tolerances. The United States Mint makes the one-cent penny with a diameter of 0.75 inches (19.05 mm), or three-quarters of an inch. Four pennies in a row equal 3.00 inches. Line up 12 US pennies flat on a table with their edges touching (12 × 0.75 inches), and the row measures an exact 9.00 inches (228.6 mm). Push the coins against the straight spine of a book or the edge of a table so the line stays straight.

3. Three standard 3x3 Post-it notes side by side

A classic square yellow Post-it note pad is cut to 3.00 inches by 3.00 inches (76.2 mm by 76.2 mm). Pull three sticky notes off the pad and stick them side by side on a desk, wall, or box so their edges touch without overlapping. Three notes at 3 inches each create a 9.00-inch strip that stays put while you mark your measurement, freeing up both of your hands.

4. Two cardboard toilet paper tubes end to end

Before throwing an empty toilet paper roll into the recycling bin, you can use it to measure 9 inches. Standard North American bathroom tissue rolls and their inner cardboard tubes are cut to 4.50 inches (114.3 mm) long so they fit standard wall-mounted toilet paper holders. Setting two cardboard tubes end to end in a straight line (4.50 + 4.50) gives you 9.00 inches. If you only have one empty tube, place it down, mark the 4.5-inch end with a pencil or your fingernail, and move the tube forward once to reach 9 inches.

5. A 6x9 brown kraft clasp envelope

In home offices and mailrooms, the standard small booklet mailer used to send half-folded documents, tax packets, or greeting booklets is the 6x9 clasp envelope. It measures 6.00 inches wide by 9.00 inches tall (152.4 mm by 228.6 mm). Because paper envelopes are die-cut from flat stock on industrial presses, the long vertical edge of a 6x9 envelope works well as a straight, flat 9-inch paper ruler that you can slide into drawer corners or lay against a box.

6. A US trade hardcover or paperback book

Book printers use fixed trim sizes that fit commercial printing presses and retail bookshelves. The standard format for most nonfiction hardcovers, biographies, textbooks, and large quality paperbacks in the United States is called US Trade, which measures 6.00 inches wide by 9.00 inches tall. Make sure you grab a full-size trade book rather than a small airport mass-market paperback, which is only 6.87 inches tall. On a 6x9 paperback, the front cover height is an exact 9.00 inches. On a hardcover book, measure the vertical height of the white paper pages inside rather than the outer cardboard cover boards, since hardcover boards overhang the pages by about 1/8 of an inch at the top and bottom.

7. A standard wall paint roller sleeve

If you have painted a bedroom or hallway, the fuzzy cylindrical roller sleeve in your garage or utility closet is 9.00 inches (228.6 mm) long. Hardware stores across North America and Europe standardize full-size wall paint rollers and their wire cage frames at 9 inches, while small trim rollers are 4 inches and wide commercial floor rollers are 18 inches. A clean 9-inch roller sleeve slipped off its wire frame gives you a rigid 9-inch cylinder.

8. Four regulation billiard (pool) balls in a row

Under World Pool-Billiard Association (WPA) and Billiard Congress of America (BCA) rules, a standard pocket billiard ball is molded from phenolic resin to a diameter of 2.25 inches (57.15 mm), within a tolerance of 0.005 inches. Because 2.25 multiplied by 4 equals 9.00, lining up four pool balls in a row with their sides touching gives you an exact 9.00-inch span. Rest the four balls against the straight rubber rail of the pool table so they do not roll apart while you measure.

9. The outer circumference of a regulation MLB baseball

Rule 3.01 of the Official Baseball Rules used by Major League Baseball states that a regulation baseball must have an outer circumference between 9.00 and 9.25 inches (229 to 235 mm). You cannot measure a flat line with a round ball directly, but you can wrap a shoelace, piece of string, or phone charging cord snugly once around the widest middle part of a baseball, pinch the spot where the string meets its starting end, and pull that string straight on a table. The pinched length of string will be 9 inches.

10. Six standard tealight candles in a row

Standard unscented white tealight candles sold in bulk bags sit inside thin aluminum or plastic cups with an outer diameter of 1.50 inches (38.1 mm) so they fit standard glass votive holders and ceramic warmers. Two tealights side by side equal 3 inches, and six standard tealights lined up edge to edge (6 × 1.50 inches) measure 9.00 inches. Check that you are using regular tealights rather than jumbo 8-hour tealights, which are 2.25 inches wide. If you happen to have jumbo tealights, four of them in a row equal 9 inches.

11. A carpenter's torpedo spirit level

Check a home tool kit or kitchen utility drawer and you will often find a small plastic or aluminum level tapered at both ends with three bubble vials for horizontal, vertical, and 45-degree checks. Toolmakers call this a torpedo level, and its standard length is 9.00 inches (228.6 mm). Plumbers, electricians, and homeowners use the 9-inch size because it fits inside narrow wall stud cavities (which leave 14.5 inches between wooden studs) and tight under-sink cabinets where a 24-inch level cannot fit.

12. A standard paper dinner plate

Disposable paper and molded-fiber plates are sold in three main grocery store sizes: 6-inch cake plates, 9-inch everyday lunch and dinner plates, and 10.25-inch heavy-duty picnic platters. A standard white uncoated paper plate or classic 9-inch Chinet dinner plate measures 9.00 inches straight across the middle from outer edge to outer edge.

13. Short locking kitchen tongs

Stainless steel spring-loaded kitchen tongs come in three standard food-service lengths: 7-inch mini tongs for ice buckets, 9-inch utility tongs for skillet cooking and air fryers, and 12-inch grill tongs for keeping your hands away from open flames. A pair of 9-inch kitchen tongs measures 9.00 inches from the top of the metal pull-ring lock to the tip of the scalloped jaws when locked shut.

14. A standard high-profile mattress box spring

In the bedding industry, the wooden or steel box spring foundation that sits under a mattress comes in two standard thicknesses: a 5-inch low-profile foundation and a 9-inch standard (or high-profile) box spring. If you pull back the bedskirt on a traditional two-piece bed set, the vertical height of a standard box spring from its bottom wood rail to its top fabric deck is 9.00 inches.

6 common household items close to 9 inches (with exact corrections)

If none of the 14 exact items are within arm's reach, you probably have a sheet of printer paper, a wallet with a bank card, paper money, a deck of cards, or a handheld game console nearby. All six of these items have fixed factory dimensions, so you can add or subtract a small, known fraction of an inch to land on 9.00 inches.

15. US Letter printer paper: 8.50 inches (add 0.50 inches)

A standard sheet of North American copy and printer paper (US Letter) measures 8.50 inches wide by 11.00 inches long (215.9 mm by 279.4 mm). You can get 9 inches from a sheet of printer paper in two ways.

First, you can use the 8.50-inch short edge and add 0.50 inches (12.7 mm). Half an inch matches the width of an average adult pinky fingernail. Hold the short edge of the paper against the object you are measuring and add one pinky fingernail width past the corner to reach 9.00 inches.

Second, you can use the 11.00-inch long edge and subtract 2.00 inches. Fold a 2-inch strip down from the top edge of the page (or place two US quarters side by side at the corner as a nearly 2-inch folding guide), and the remaining height of the paper is 9.00 inches. If you live outside North America and use ISO A4 printer paper, the sheet measures 8.27 inches by 11.69 inches, which means the short edge is 0.73 inches shy of 9 inches.

16. A Nintendo Switch with Joy-Cons attached: 9.40 inches (subtract 0.40 inches)

Both the original Nintendo Switch and the Nintendo Switch OLED model share the exact same horizontal length when the left and right Joy-Con controllers are clicked onto the side rails: 9.40 inches (239 mm). That makes the console 0.40 inches (10.4 mm) longer than 9 inches. Four-tenths of an inch is a hair under half an inch, which matches the width of one rubber analog thumbstick cap on the controller. Measure from the far-left edge of the left Joy-Con and stop just inside the curved outer plastic rim of the right Joy-Con (one thumbstick width back from the edge) to get 9.00 inches.

17. A No. 10 white business envelope: 9.50 inches (subtract 0.50 inches)

The long white rectangular envelope used for mailing checks, invoices, and trifolded 8.5x11 letters is a No. 10 envelope. Postal standards set the size of a No. 10 envelope at 4.125 inches tall by 9.50 inches long (104.8 mm by 241.3 mm). Envelope makers build it half an inch wider than an 8.5-inch sheet of US Letter paper so folded letters slide in without catching on the corners. Lay the long edge of the envelope along your object and stop half an inch (one pinky fingernail width) before the right corner to mark 9.00 inches.

18. One and a half US dollar bills: 9.21 inches (subtract 0.21 inches)

Every denomination of modern US paper currency ($1, $2, $5, $10, $20, $50, and $100 bills) is trimmed to 6.14 inches long by 2.61 inches wide (156.0 mm by 66.3 mm). Lay one flat bill on a table (6.14 inches), fold a second bill in half lengthwise so it measures 3.07 inches, and line them up end to end. Together they span 9.21 inches (233.9 mm), which is 0.21 inches (5.3 mm) past 9 inches. Look at the narrow unprinted white paper margin outside the green ink border on the short end of a dollar bill. That white margin is roughly 0.20 inches wide. Overlap the two bills by the width of that single white border strip, and their combined length is 9.00 inches.

19. Two and a half credit cards: 8.86 inches (add 0.14 inches)

Every bank card, credit card, driver's license, and hotel keycard follows the international ISO/IEC 7810 ID-1 standard, which sets the card size at 3.370 inches long by 2.125 inches wide (85.60 mm by 53.98 mm) and 0.03 inches (0.76 mm) thick.

To measure 9 inches with cards from your wallet, lay two cards end to end along their long edges (3.370 × 2 = 6.740 inches), then add a third card turned sideways along its short width (+ 2.125 inches). That three-card line measures 8.865 inches (225.17 mm). You are 0.135 inches (3.43 mm) short of 9.00 inches, or about one-eighth of an inch. If you stack four credit cards flat on top of each other, their combined thickness (4 × 0.03 inches = 0.12 inches) bridges that small remaining gap to give you 9.00 inches.

20. Two and a half poker playing cards: 9.50 inches (subtract 0.50 inches)

A standard poker-size playing card (such as a Bicycle deck, UNO card, or trading card game sleeve) measures 2.50 inches wide by 3.50 inches tall (63.5 mm by 88.9 mm). Laying two playing cards end to end vertically along their 3.50-inch tall edges gives you 7.00 inches. Adding a third card turned sideways along its 2.50-inch width brings the line to 9.50 inches. Subtract half an inch (one pinky fingernail width) from the end of the third card to hit 9.00 inches.

If you have a narrower bridge-size deck of playing cards (2.25 inches wide by 3.50 inches tall), lining up four bridge cards sideways along their 2.25-inch widths (4 × 2.25 inches) equals an exact 9.00 inches without any adjustment.

How to measure 9 inches with your hands and body

When you are standing in a store aisle or reading a restaurant menu with nothing in your pockets, you can use your hands or feet to estimate 9 inches within a quarter of an inch.

1. The stretched adult hand span (thumb tip to pinky tip)

A hand span is the distance from the outer tip of your thumb to the outer tip of your pinky finger when you press your hand flat and spread your fingers as wide as they will go. For an average adult man, a fully stretched hand span measures between 8.5 and 9.5 inches (21.6 to 24.1 cm), with the average sitting right around 9.0 inches. For an average adult woman, a stretched hand span measures between 7.5 and 8.5 inches (19.0 to 21.6 cm), so an average woman's hand span plus the width of one thumb equals roughly 9.0 inches.

Spread your hand flat against the 9-inch virtual ruler at the top of this page and see where your pinky tip lands. Once you know whether your personal hand span hits 8.25 inches, 8.75 inches, or 9.0 inches, you have a built-in 9-inch reference wherever you go.

2. Three adult index fingers end to end

For most adults, the index finger measures about 3.0 inches (7.6 cm) from the bottom knuckle crease where the finger meets the palm to the tip of the fingernail. Placing your index finger down three times end to end along a flat surface (3 × 3 inches) gives you a quick 9-inch estimate.

3. Wrist crease to inner elbow (forearm check)

On an average-height adult, the inside of the forearm from the wrist crease to the bend of the inner elbow measures between 9.5 and 10.5 inches. If you hold an item against your inner wrist crease and it stops about one inch (one thumb width) short of the crease of your elbow, that item is right around 9 inches long.

4. Bare foot length and shoe sizes (Brannock Device standard)

If you are shopping for shoes online or measuring insoles, a bare foot that measures 9.00 inches (22.86 cm) from the back of the heel to the tip of the longest toe matches these standard shoe sizes on a store Brannock Device:

  • US Women's shoe size: 6 to 6.5
  • US Men's shoe size: 5
  • Youth / Big Kid shoe size: 4.5 to 5
  • UK shoe size: 4 to 4.5
  • European (EU) shoe size: 37
  • Mondopoint (ski boot and military sizing): 230 (228.6 mm)

Keep in mind that the outer rubber sole of a sneaker or boot adds 0.75 to 1.25 inches of material around the foot. While a Women's Size 6.5 foot is 9 inches long inside the shoe, the outer sole of that shoe measures roughly 10 inches along the bottom tread. For the outside of a shoe to measure 9 inches, you would be looking at a Youth Size 3 sneaker or a slim Women's Size 5 flat.

How to get a true 9-inch measurement on your screen

Web browsers cannot detect the physical dimensions of your monitor or phone glass on their own. Instead, a browser draws inches using PPI (Pixels Per Inch), and physical screens range from 81 PPI on a 27-inch desktop monitor up to 460 PPI on a modern phone.

Run through these four steps so the 9-inch ruler on your screen matches a metal tape measure:

  1. Reset your browser zoom to 100%. If you zoomed in on a site earlier to read small text, your browser stretches the ruler markings by the same percentage. Press Ctrl + 0 on Windows, Linux, or Chromebook, or Cmd + 0 on a Mac, to return your browser zoom to 100%.
  2. Check the scale with a physical bank card. Grab any standard credit card, debit card, or driver's license (3.37 inches long by 2.125 inches wide). Hold the long edge of the card flat against your screen starting at the 0-inch mark of the virtual ruler. The right edge of your card should line up with 3 and 3/8 inches (3.37 inches). If it looks off, click the calibration button above the article and adjust the slider until the on-screen card matches your physical card.
  3. Look straight down at the glass. Screen glass puts a thin gap between the outer surface you touch and the lit pixel panel underneath. Looking at the screen from a side angle creates parallax error that can shift your reading by 1/8 of an inch. Close one eye and look straight on at a 90-degree angle over the 0-inch mark and then directly over the 9-inch mark.
  4. Account for smaller smartphone screens. Most phones have a diagonal screen size between 6.1 and 6.9 inches, meaning the long vertical edge of the glass is only 5.5 to 6.2 inches long. Because a 6-inch phone display cannot fit all 9 inches on screen at once without shrinking the scale, keep the ruler at 1:1 actual size and either measure in two steps (mark the 4.5-inch halfway point on your object first, then measure the second 4.5 inches) or open this page on a tablet, laptop, or desktop monitor where all 9 inches fit across the screen in one view.

Frequently asked questions about 9 inches

How many centimeters is 9 inches?

Nine inches is 22.86 centimeters. One international inch is defined as 2.54 centimeters, so multiplying 9 by 2.54 gives 22.86 cm (9 × 2.54 = 22.86 cm).

How many millimeters is 9 inches?

Nine inches equals 228.6 millimeters. One inch contains 25.4 millimeters (9 × 25.4 = 228.6 mm), which is the standard unit used on 3D printing beds, furniture hardware, and engineering drawings.

How many feet is 9 inches?

Nine inches is 0.75 feet, or three-quarters (3/4) of a foot. Since 1 foot has 12 inches, divide 9 by 12 (9 ÷ 12 = 0.75 ft). That puts 9 inches at 3 inches shorter than a 1-foot ruler.

Is a 9-inch sub sandwich big enough for one person?

Yes. A 9-inch sub sandwich is a full single-person meal that sits right between a light 6-inch sub and a heavy 12-inch footlong. A 9-inch chicken or turkey sub has 50% more bread, meat, and toppings than a 6-inch sub, usually landing between 650 and 900 calories before chips or a drink.

How many slices and servings are in a 9-inch pizza or 9-inch cake?

A 9-inch pizza has a surface area of 63.6 square inches and is usually cut into 4 or 6 slices to feed 1 to 2 people. A 9-inch two-layer birthday cake is much taller and denser, yielding 12 to 16 party slices, while a 9-inch fruit or pumpkin pie is cut into 8 standard wedges.

Is 9 inches bigger than 20 cm?

Yes, 9 inches is 2.86 centimeters (about 1 and 1/8 inches) longer than 20 centimeters. Twenty centimeters equals 7.87 inches, while 9 inches equals 22.86 centimeters.

How big is a 9x9 square pan compared to a 9-inch round pan?

A 9-inch round cake pan (2 inches deep) holds 8 cups (2 quarts) of batter and has a surface area of 63.6 square inches (π × 4.5²). A 9x9-inch square baking pan (2 inches deep) holds 10 cups (2.5 quarts) and has a surface area of 81 square inches (9 × 9). Because a 9x9 square pan has 27% more surface area than a 9-inch round pan, batter baked in a 9-inch round pan sits deeper and takes 5 to 8 minutes longer to bake through than the same recipe in a 9x9 square pan.

How does a 9-inch tablet compare to an iPad?

Manufacturers measure tablet screens diagonally from corner to corner across the lit display panel, excluding the outer frame. A 9-inch tablet sits between an iPad Mini (which has an 8.3-inch diagonal screen and stands 7.69 inches tall overall) and a standard 11-inch iPad (which has a 10.9-inch diagonal screen and stands 9.79 inches tall overall). On a full-size iPad, the outer physical height of the casing is 9.79 inches, making the tablet about three-quarters of an inch taller than 9 inches.
